## Abstract

This thesis challenges the dominant view that accuracy is the primary quality
criterion for estimation in time-based IT consulting. Drawing on a qualitative
single-case study of Sopra Steria, it argues that estimation in this setting is
better evaluated by **defensibility**: whether an estimate can be justified as
credible, manageable, and organisationally acceptable at the time of commitment.

Time-based consulting requires firms to commit consultants, competence, and
price before scope, technical complexity, and customer decisions are settled.
Weak estimates therefore do more than produce inaccurate forecasts: they distort
staffing, erode margins, and damage customer relationships. Based on six
semi-structured interviews with professionals involved in commercial estimation,
staffing, and delivery-related decision-making, the thesis develops the concept
of **controlled commitment**.

Controlled commitment describes how decision-makers commit enough to make an
opportunity actionable while limiting exposure to unclear scope, unstable
customer needs, scarce competence, and weak commercial conditions. The thesis
shows that assignments are themselves partly shaped by estimation, that
time-based contracts relocate rather than remove supplier commitment, and that
staffing proposals function both as competence claims and risk allocations. By
repositioning defensibility as the operative criterion for quality, the thesis
challenges the assumption that estimated and actual effort are commensurable
measures of estimation quality in knowledge-intensive, time-based work.

## Key contributions

- **Defensibility over accuracy** — reframes estimation quality around whether an estimate is credible, manageable, and organisationally acceptable at the moment of commitment, rather than how closely it matches actual effort.
- **The concept of _controlled commitment_** — a framework for how decision-makers commit enough to act on an opportunity while limiting exposure to unclear scope, unstable customer needs, scarce competence, and weak commercial conditions.
- **Estimation shapes the assignment** — assignments are not fixed inputs but are themselves partly produced by the estimation process.
- **Contracts relocate, not remove, risk** — time-based contracts shift supplier commitment rather than eliminating it.
- **Staffing as claim and risk allocation** — staffing proposals operate simultaneously as competence claims and as allocations of risk.
